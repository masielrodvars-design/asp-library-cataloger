Foundation 2

# The Living Library Knowledge Model

## What Should Every Book Be Able to Tell Us?

The knowledge framework for every Book Profile in the MFEE Learning Center Library.

Every Book Profile is a living body of knowledge that grows through relationships.

Chapter 1 — Foundations
Purpose

The Living Library Knowledge Model defines the knowledge that the MFEE Learning Center Library maintains about every book.

It serves as the foundation for cataloging, discovery, teaching, tutoring, recommendation, and community contribution.

Unlike a traditional library catalog, which focuses primarily on identifying and locating books, the Living Library Knowledge Model is designed to help every book tell its complete story.

Each Book Profile captures not only bibliographic information, but also educational value, instructional possibilities, community experiences, and the relationships that develop around the book over time.

The Knowledge Model provides a single source of truth that supports librarians, educators, tutors, volunteers, families, students, artificial intelligence, and future software systems.

What Should Every Book Be Able to Tell Us?

Traditional library catalogs answer important questions.

Who wrote this book?
When was it published?
Where can I find it?

Those questions matter.

But they are not enough.

The Living Library believes every book has more to share.

Every book can teach us something.

Support someone.

Inspire someone.

Connect people.

Create memories.

Strengthen relationships.

Its story grows every time it is read, shared, taught, recommended, donated, or rediscovered.

The purpose of the Living Library Knowledge Model is to preserve that knowledge.

Every book in the MFEE Learning Center Library has a living profile that grows over time.

Some parts of that profile remain constant.

Others become richer because of the community that surrounds the book.

Together they tell the complete story of the book within the Living Library.

What is a Book Profile?

Every book in the MFEE Learning Center Library has a Book Profile.

A Book Profile is more than a catalog record.

It is the complete body of knowledge the Living Library maintains about a single book.

Some parts of a Book Profile never change.

The title.

The author.

The ISBN.

Other parts grow richer over time.

Teacher recommendations.

Student reflections.

Instructional ideas.

Community experiences.

Together, these create a living profile that evolves as the book becomes part of the life of the Learning Center.

The Living Library preserves both.

The facts that identify a book.

And the experiences that give it meaning.

Four Sources of Knowledge

No single person—or system—knows everything about a book.

The Living Library recognizes four complementary sources of knowledge.

Each contributes something unique.

Together they create a more complete understanding than any one source could provide alone.

1. Bibliographic Knowledge

Bibliographic Knowledge identifies the book.

It answers questions such as:

What is this book called?
Who created it?
When was it published?
Which edition is this?

Examples include:

Title
Author
Illustrator
ISBN
Publisher
Publication Date
Series
Edition

This knowledge provides the permanent identity of the book.

2. Librarian Knowledge

Librarian Knowledge organizes the collection.

It reflects the professional judgment of librarians and those responsible for maintaining the library.

Examples include:

Collection assignment
Call number
Shelving location
Catalog corrections
Reading level verification
Collection maintenance notes

This knowledge helps every reader find the right book.

3. AI Knowledge

AI Knowledge supports discovery.

Artificial intelligence analyzes books to identify patterns that help readers, teachers, tutors, and families discover books that meet their needs.

Examples include:

Themes
Subjects
Representation
Instructional supports
Suggested audience
Short description
Discovery keywords

AI contributes recommendations.

People review, refine, and approve them.

The Living Library values AI as a partner in discovery—not as a replacement for human judgment.

4. Community Knowledge

Community Knowledge is what makes the Living Library unique.

It captures the experiences that grow around books as they are read, shared, discussed, taught, and loved.

Possible contributors include:

Students
Teen tutors
Teachers
Families
Volunteers
Librarians
Guest readers
Community partners

Examples include:

Student recommendations
Teacher notes
Tutor insights
Family reflections
Classroom activities
Favorite read-alouds
Program connections
Conversation starters

Community Knowledge transforms a collection of books into a living community resource.

Three Types of Knowledge

Not all knowledge changes at the same pace.

The Living Library recognizes three types of knowledge within every Book Profile.

Permanent Knowledge

Permanent Knowledge rarely changes.

Examples include:

Title
Author
ISBN
Publisher
Publication Year

This knowledge forms the identity of the book.

Curated Knowledge

Curated Knowledge is intentionally maintained by librarians, educators, and AI.

It may evolve as the collection grows or educational practices change.

Examples include:

Collection
Call Number
Reading Level
Subjects
Themes
Representation
Instructional Supports

Curated Knowledge helps readers discover books intentionally and consistently.

Living Knowledge

Living Knowledge grows through the experiences of the community.

It reflects how books are actually used and remembered.

Examples include:

Student recommendations
Teacher reflections
Tutor notes
Family stories
Community favorites
Program experiences

Living Knowledge is never finished.

It grows every time someone reads, teaches, recommends, donates, or shares a book.

Chapter 2 — The Ten Dimensions of a Book Profile

Every Book Profile is organized into ten dimensions.

Each dimension answers a different question.

Together they create a complete picture of the book.

Dimension	Guiding Question
1. Library Classification	Where does this book belong?
2. Bibliographic Identity	What is this book?
3. Reader Profile	Who is this book for?
4. Subjects	What is this book about?
5. Themes	What ideas does this book explore?
6. Representation	Whose experiences are represented?
7. Instructional Supports	How can this book support learning?
8. Discovery	How might someone discover this book?
9. AI Insights	What has AI learned about this book?
10. Community Voices	What has our community learned from this book?

Each dimension contributes a unique perspective.

Together they form a complete Book Profile that supports discovery, learning, stewardship, and community.

Dimension 1 — Library Classification
Guiding Question

Where does this book belong?

Purpose

Library Classification provides the organizational structure that enables every book to be located, managed, and returned to the appropriate place within the MFEE Learning Center Library.

Its primary purpose is stewardship.

A well-designed classification system allows the community to care for the collection so that every reader can find and enjoy the books they need.

Classification creates order.

That order makes discovery possible.

Why It Matters

A book that cannot be found cannot be read.

Library Classification ensures that books remain accessible to everyone who uses the Learning Center.

It supports:

Daily browsing
Shelving
Inventory
Volunteer training
Collection management
Future growth

Although readers may never think about the classification system, it quietly supports every successful interaction with the library.

Primary Users

Library Classification primarily serves:

Librarians
Volunteers
Learning Center staff
Inventory managers

It also indirectly supports every student, teacher, tutor, and family by ensuring that books can be found consistently.

Primary Knowledge Source

Librarian Knowledge

Artificial intelligence may recommend a collection assignment.

Final responsibility belongs to the people who care for the collection.

Knowledge Type

Curated Knowledge

Library Classification is intentionally maintained and evolves as the collection grows.

Information Included

Library Classification may include:

Collection
Collection Code
Call Number
Physical Location
Shelf
Language Collection
Format Collection
Special Collections

Together these elements answer one simple question:

Where should someone look for this book?

Future Data Fields

Potential implementation fields include:

Collection Code
Collection Name
Call Number
Shelf
Language
Format
Inventory Status

These implementation details may evolve without changing the purpose of Library Classification.

Example

Collection: Spanish Juvenile Fiction

Collection Code: SP J

Call Number: SP J CAM

Shelf: Spanish Picture Books

Design Principles

Library Classification should be:

Consistent
Easy to understand
Easy to maintain
Independent of software
Scalable as the collection grows

Classification exists to support people—not to make the system more complicated.

Relationship to Other Dimensions

Library Classification answers:

Where is the book?

The remaining dimensions answer different questions.

Reader Profile explains who the book is for.
Subjects explain what the book is about.
Themes explain what ideas it explores.
Discovery explains how someone might find it.
Community Voices explain how the community has experienced it.

Together they create a complete Book Profile.

Living Library Principle

The Living Library distinguishes between finding a book and discovering a book.

Library Classification helps readers locate a book once they know what they are looking for.

The remaining dimensions help readers discover books they did not yet know they needed.

Both are essential.

Together they transform a library from a storage system into a relationship-centered learning ecosystem.

Looking Ahead

The remaining nine dimensions build upon this same framework.

Each answers a different question.

Together they form a complete Book Profile that supports librarians, educators, tutors, families, volunteers, students, artificial intelligence, and future software systems.

The Living Library does not simply describe books.

It preserves the knowledge, relationships, and community experiences that help every reader discover the right book at the right moment.

Every Book Profile is a living body of knowledge that grows through relationships.
