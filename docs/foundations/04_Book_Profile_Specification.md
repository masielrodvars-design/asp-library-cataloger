Book Profile Specification
How Should Someone Experience a Book?
Designing every Book Profile to inspire discovery, learning, and connection.

A Book Profile should introduce a reader to a book before it asks them to find it.

Chapter 1 — Purpose
Why This Foundation Exists

The Book Profile is the heart of the Living Library.

It is the primary way readers, educators, tutors, volunteers, families, librarians, and artificial intelligence interact with a book.

A traditional catalog record is designed to identify and locate a book.

A Living Library Book Profile is designed to introduce a reader to a book.

It answers practical questions such as:

What is this book?
Where can I find it?

But it also answers more meaningful questions.

Why might I love this book?
Who is it for?
How can I use it?
What has our community learned from it?
What should I read next?

A Book Profile transforms information into understanding.

Its purpose is not simply to describe a book.

Its purpose is to help create a connection between a reader and a story.

Design Principle

A Book Profile should be read from top to bottom like meeting someone for the first time.

Each section should answer the next natural question a reader might ask.

Chapter 2 — The Reader Journey

When someone opens a Book Profile, they naturally ask questions in a particular order.

The Living Library is designed to answer those questions in a way that feels intuitive and welcoming.

Reader Question	Book Profile Section
What is this book?	Identity
Why should I care?	Summary
Is this book right for me?	Reader Profile
What is it about?	Subjects
What ideas does it explore?	Themes
How could I use it?	Instructional Supports
What do others think?	Community Voices
Where can I find it?	Library Classification
What should I read next?	Related Books


Notice that finding the book comes after discovering the book.

That ordering reflects the philosophy of the Living Library.

Chapter 3 — The Anatomy of a Book Profile
A Book Profile should tell a story.

Every section should answer the next question a reader naturally asks.

The profile should gradually move from curiosity to understanding, then from understanding to action.

A reader should finish a Book Profile knowing:

what the book is,
why it matters,
whether it is right for them,
and where to find it.
The Ideal Reading Experience

Rather than presenting information as a database record, the Living Library organizes every Book Profile as a journey.

1. Meet the Book

The reader is introduced to the book.

This section should immediately spark curiosity.

It includes:

Cover Image
Title
Author
One-sentence description
Language
Format

The goal is simple:

"Tell me what this book is."

2. Discover Why It Matters

Once the reader knows what the book is, they naturally ask:

"Why should I read it?"

This section provides a short, inviting description written for people—not librarians.

It explains:

Why the story matters
What makes it memorable
What kind of reader might enjoy it

The tone should feel like a trusted recommendation.

Not a catalog entry.

3. Decide If It's Right for Me

Now the reader asks:

"Is this my kind of book?"

The Reader Profile answers that question.

Examples include:

Age Range
Grade Levels
Reading Level
Independent Reading
Read Aloud
Family Reading
Beginning English Learners
Advanced Readers

This section helps readers quickly recognize whether the book matches their needs.

4. Understand the Book

Now the reader wants to know more.

This section combines:

Subjects
Themes
Representation

Together these help readers understand both the content of the story and the ideas it explores.

5. Imagine Using the Book

Different readers use books in different ways.

This section helps readers imagine possibilities.

Teachers might see:

Read Aloud
Writing Mentor Text
Science Connections

Families might notice:

Bedtime Reading
Conversation Starter

Tutors might discover:

Vocabulary Support
Fluency Practice

The same book becomes useful in different ways for different people.

6. Hear the Community

This is where the Living Library becomes truly alive.

Readers discover recommendations from people they trust.

A younger student might think:

"Max loved this book. I want to read it too."

A teacher might notice:

"Three tutors used this successfully with reluctant readers."

A family might read:

"We laughed through every chapter."

Community Voices help books travel through relationships.

7. Find the Book

Only now do we answer:

"Where is it?"

This section includes:

Collection
Call Number
Shelf
Language
Availability

Finding comes after discovering.

8. Continue the Journey

Every Book Profile should encourage another discovery.

Related Books may connect through:

Themes
Subjects
Authors
Series
Community recommendations
AI recommendations

The Living Library believes every great book leads to another.

Chapter 4 — Designing for Different People

One Book Profile.

Many experiences.

The information stays the same.

The presentation changes.

Student Experience

Simple.

Visual.

Encouraging.

Students should leave thinking:

"I want to read this!"

Teacher Experience

Teachers are planning learning.

Their view highlights:

Themes
Instructional Supports
Related Books
Community teaching ideas
Tutor Experience

Tutors are building confidence.

Their view emphasizes:

Reading level
Engagement strategies
Vocabulary support
Student recommendations
Family Experience

Families are building memories.

Their view highlights:

Shared reading
Conversation starters
Similar family favorites
Books to read together
Librarian Experience

Librarians are caring for the collection.

Their view emphasizes:

Classification
Inventory
Cataloging
Collection management

Every audience encounters the same Book Profile.

Each simply sees it through a different lens.

People choose books because they are inspired, not because they know the call number.
