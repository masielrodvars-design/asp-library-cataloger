# Living Library Design Principles

**Version:** 1.0

**Status:** Living Document

**Steward:** MFEE Learning Center

**Last Updated:** August 2026

---

# Purpose

The Living Library Design Principles provide a shared framework for making decisions about the software, knowledge model, community experience, and future growth of the MFEE Living Library.

Whenever new features, vocabulary, workflows, or technologies are considered, these principles should guide decision-making.

Technology will evolve.

These principles should endure.

---

# Principle 1

## Discovery Before Classification

Traditional libraries organize books.

The Living Library helps people discover them.

Every design decision should strengthen discovery rather than simply improve organization.

---

# Principle 2

## Books Are Discovered Through Relationships

Readers discover books through many kinds of relationships.

Relationships between:

- people
- ideas
- experiences
- communities
- learning
- stories

The Living Library should strengthen these connections.

---

# Principle 3

## Knowledge Belongs to the Community

The knowledge of the Living Library belongs to the Learning Center community.

Teachers, tutors, families, volunteers, and students continually shape how books are understood.

The software exists to preserve, organize, and share that knowledge—not replace it.

---

# Principle 4

## Simple for People. Sophisticated by Design. Built to Grow.

The Living Library should remain intuitive for the people who use it while supporting increasingly sophisticated capabilities behind the scenes.

As the project evolves, complexity should be absorbed by the software—not by the user.

Growth should happen through richer knowledge, thoughtful architecture, and better discovery rather than more complicated workflows.

---

# Principle 5

## Richer Vocabulary Before More Data Fields

The Living Library uses a small number of well-defined discovery dimensions, each supported by a rich, evolving vocabulary.

When new ideas emerge, the first question should not be:

> "Should we add another field?"

Instead, we ask:

> "Can this idea be expressed through the language we already share?"

This keeps the Living Library understandable while allowing its knowledge to evolve.

---

---

# Principle 6

## Build Library Literacy Through Discovery

The Living Library serves two complementary purposes.

First, it helps children develop the confidence and skills to navigate libraries independently by using familiar organizational systems such as picture books, early readers, graphic novels, chapter books, and the Dewey Decimal System for nonfiction.

Second, it inspires joyful discovery through thoughtfully curated collections, rotating displays, and community recommendations that invite readers to explore books through ideas, experiences, and relationships.

These two approaches are not competing philosophies.

They work together.

Permanent organization teaches children how libraries work.

Curated collections help children discover why libraries matter.

The Living Library prepares children not only to use libraries, but to love them.

# Principle 7

## Build for the Next Steward

Every design decision should make the Living Library easier for future staff, volunteers, educators, and developers to understand and improve.

The project should outlive any individual contributor.

Documentation, software, and knowledge should remain accessible, transparent, and maintainable.

---

# A Living Document

These principles are expected to evolve as the Living Library grows.

Changes should be thoughtful and infrequent.

The goal is not to document every decision.

The goal is to preserve the values that guide them.
