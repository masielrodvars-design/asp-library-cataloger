# ADR-003: Multiple Views of Shared Knowledge

**Status:** Accepted

**Date:** August 2026

---

# Context

The Living Library serves multiple audiences with different needs.

Children, families, tutors, and educators are primarily interested in discovering books.

Volunteers and staff responsible for maintaining the library also need practical information for locating, shelving, and managing the collection.

A traditional library catalog often presents the same information to every user.

The Living Library instead recognizes that different audiences should experience the same underlying knowledge in different ways.

---

# Decision

The Living Library will maintain a single source of truth for every book.

From that shared Book Knowledge Record, the system will present different experiences based on the needs of the user.

The primary experiences include:

- Reader Experience
- Library Learning Experience
- Collection Management Experience

These experiences share the same underlying data while emphasizing different information.

---

# Reader Experience

The Reader Experience is designed to inspire discovery and meaningful connections with books.

Examples include:

- Why This Book Matters
- Themes
- Subjects
- Curated Collections
- Community Voices
- Related Books
- Reading Recommendations

The goal is to help readers discover books that are meaningful to them.

---

# Library Learning Experience

The Living Library is also an educational tool.

Rather than hiding library organization, it teaches children how libraries work.

Every Book Story should help readers learn how to independently locate books within the Learning Center and transfer those skills to public and school libraries.

Examples include:

- Section
- Call Number
- Dewey Decimal Number (when applicable)
- Shelf Location
- Library Tips
- Nearby Books
- Browse Suggestions

For example:

> Books with similar call numbers are often located near one another.

> Explore the books nearby—you may discover another favorite.

The goal is not simply to help readers find one book, but to help them become confident lifelong library users.

---

# Collection Management Experience

Staff and volunteers require additional information for maintaining the collection.

Examples include:

- Barcode
- Inventory Status
- Number of Copies
- Cataloging History
- Review Status
- Storage Information

These tools support collection management without adding unnecessary complexity to the reader experience.

---

# Rationale

Maintaining a single Book Knowledge Record while presenting multiple user experiences creates a system that is both simple and powerful.

Readers receive an inviting, discovery-centered experience.

Children simultaneously learn transferable library skills.

Staff receive the operational information needed to manage the collection efficiently.

This approach eliminates duplicate records while supporting multiple educational and operational goals.

---

# Consequences

A single Book Knowledge Record can support:

- Book Stories
- Search Results
- Curated Collections
- QR Code Pages
- Reader Recommendations
- Library Learning
- Collection Management

The Living Library therefore becomes more than a catalog.

It becomes both a discovery platform and a teaching tool that helps children build confidence using libraries throughout their lives.
